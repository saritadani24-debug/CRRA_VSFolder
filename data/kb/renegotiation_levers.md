# Renegotiation Levers

## Price uplift benchmarks

A renewal price increase (uplift) up to 7% per year is considered in line with market inflation for software and services and may be accepted without challenge.

An uplift between 7% and 15% must be challenged with a benchmark from at least one comparable vendor or an internal price history.

An uplift above 15% must not be accepted as proposed. The contract must be renegotiated, and if the vendor will not move, the renewal is escalated to the next approval band's approver with the benchmark evidence attached.

## Levers to trade for a discount

Where a price reduction or a lower uplift is sought, the following levers may be offered to the vendor in exchange:

- A longer term commitment (two or three years) with a capped annual uplift.
- Annual prepayment instead of quarterly or monthly billing, subject to Finance review.
- Consolidating additional business units or adjacent products onto the same agreement.
- Acting as a public reference or case study, where Marketing agrees.

Levers must not be offered that commit spend beyond the approval authority of the person negotiating.

## Right-sizing underused licences

Licence utilisation is measured as active seats divided by purchased seats. Where utilisation is below 70%, the renewal must be renegotiated to reduce the seat count to the active number plus a 10% buffer before any price discussion begins.

Where utilisation is below 40%, the business owner must also confirm whether the capability is still required, and the contract should be considered for consolidation or termination rather than simple renewal.
